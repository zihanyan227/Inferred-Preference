Inferred Preference Simulator

Simulate participants in the experiment. Each one watches A's decision under every K × W condition and rates how much A likes brownie and how much A likes cupcake. Three groups of participants answer as the three candidate models would, plus response noise.

Task

A box holds 2 brownies and 2 cupcakes. A keeps 2 and B gets the rest. The participant is told what A knows about B (K) and how much A cares about B (W), sees what A kept, then rates on two 0–100 sliders: how much does A like brownie? and how much does A like cupcake?

27 trials per participant: 3 K × 3 W × 3 decisions

All 27 trials

Simulated mean rating, brownie / cupcake, out of 100. Noise-free model predictions are in brackets.

Bayesian participants: P(RA | d, K, W) ∝ P(d | RA, K, W) · P(RA | K, W), with U(d) = wA·RA(A's share) + wB·EK[RB(B's share)] and P(d) ∝ exp(β·U). W is known only as a condition; the likelihood is averaged over care values inside it. Their ratings are 100·E[rbrownie] and 100·E[rcupcake] under the posterior; the prior over RA is uniform on an 11 × 11 grid. Heuristic participants (Kelley's discounting / augmentation): pref = clip(choice + γ·sW·k, −1, 1), with k = +1, −1, 0 for B prefers brownie, cupcake, unknown and sW = 0, 0.5, 1 for A cares more about self, equally, more about B. Choice-as-preference participants: pref = choice. For these two models the ratings are 50 + 50·pref (brownie) and 50 − 50·pref (cupcake). Every rating gets the participant's offset plus Gaussian noise, then is rounded and clipped to 0–100.
